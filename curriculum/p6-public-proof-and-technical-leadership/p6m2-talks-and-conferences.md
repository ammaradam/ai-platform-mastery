# P6M2 · Talks and conferences

**Pillar:** [Public proof and technical leadership](README.md) · **Time:** ~6 h · **Pass mark:** 80% on the quiz

## Learning objectives

- Write a talk proposal (CFP) that gets accepted
- Structure a 25-minute technical talk
- Build a talk pipeline from meetups to international conferences

## Lesson

## Where to speak (ladder)
Local meetups (PyData KL, Kubernetes/CNCF KL, GDG, AWS/Azure user groups) →
PyCon MY / regional conferences (PyCon APAC, KubeCon + CloudNativeCon events,
MLOps-focused conferences) → global conferences. You've spoken at PyCon
Malaysia — you're already on the ladder.

## Writing a CFP that works
Reviewers read hundreds. Make theirs easy:
- **Title** — specific, promises a takeaway ("What 100 agent traces taught us
  about evals in Manglish").
- **Abstract** — problem, why it matters, what you'll show, what attendees
  leave with. Concrete beats clever.
- **Audience and level** stated clearly.
- **Evidence you can deliver** — real system, real numbers, links to prior
  talks or posts.
- Original material: talks about your own production experience beat survey
  talks.

## Structuring a 25-minute talk
1. Hook: the problem in one story (2 min).
2. Context: just enough background (3 min).
3. 3 key points max, each with evidence or a demo (15 min).
4. What to do Monday morning (3 min).
5. Resources slide (2 min).
Rehearse out loud at least three times; time it; cut 20%.

## Reuse
One body of work → blog post → meetup talk → conference talk → video → posts.

## Quiz

Answer without notes first. Answers are in the collapsible section below.

**Q1.** Which talk title is most likely to be accepted?

- A) "AI: The Future"
- B) "An Introduction to MLOps"
- C) "How we cut model release time from two weeks to two days at an auction company"
- D) "Kubernetes Tips"

**Q2.** What should a strong abstract always state?

- A) All tools used
- B) Your job title
- C) What attendees will leave with
- D) Your company's revenue

**Q3.** How many key points should a 25-minute talk have?

- A) 1 slide
- B) As many as possible
- C) 10+
- D) About 3

**Q4.** After rehearsing, your talk runs 30 minutes for a 25-minute slot. Best fix?

- A) Talk faster
- B) Cut content by around 20%
- C) Skip Q&A
- D) Remove the demo always

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

- **Q1: C** — Specific, experience-based, with a clear takeaway.
- **Q2: C** — Reviewers select for audience value.
- **Q3: D** — Three well-supported points are remembered; ten are not.
- **Q4: B** — Cutting is better than rushing.

</details>

## Exercises

### Submit a CFP (core)

Write and submit a talk proposal to a meetup or conference based on Pillar 4 or Pillar 3 work.

**Deliverable:** Submitted abstract (save the text in the repo).

**Done when:**
- [ ] Title has a specific takeaway; abstract under 300 words

### Deliver a meetup talk (core)

Give a 20-25 minute talk at a local meetup; record it if allowed.

**Deliverable:** Slides + recording link.

**Done when:**
- [ ] Rehearsed and timed at least 3 times
